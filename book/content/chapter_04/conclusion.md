## Conclusion

In this chapter, we set out to develop a comprehensive understanding of the point of convective onset as a function of the heat production rate, the domain curvature, the half-cellular aspect ratio, and an exponential temperature-dependent viscosity parameter. To this end, we designed and executed two separate experiments, both powered by our *Everest* toolkit. The first experiment - our Generalised Eigenvector analysis - conclusively demonstrated that the approximation commonly used for $\alpha_\mathrm{cr}$ in the literature is not reliable for even modest curvatures. Our second experiment confirmed the results of the first experiment via a totally independent methodology, and then went beyond the question of curvature to explore the effects of heating and viscosity variation. This second experiment succeeded in producing highly accurate synthetic data models that allow the point of convective onset to be predicted quickly, cheaply, and precisely, without explicit modelling. These synthetic data models are also suggestive of the physics underlying these systems, and we look forward to revisiting them for that purpose in the future. Unfortunately, we were not successful in producing a reliable model for the most complexly multi-dependent cases, due to sparse data coverage in the computationally costly high-$\eta_\Delta$ part of parameter space. However, we are confident that the methodology we have developed and demonstrated here will be sufficient to attain that final objective as soon as our automated modelling pipeline fills in those remaining gaps.

While the study of convective onset is interesting in itself, one of our motives in conducting this experiment was to provide a testbed for the development of advanced suite modelling techniques. These are the techniques we hope to employ to make sense of our very much larger finite-amplitude mantle convection dataset, which is the subject of the remainder of this thesis.

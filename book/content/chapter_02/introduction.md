# Foundations

This research program is predicated on the notion that the solid Earth can be understood as a viscous convecting fluid.

In the first chapter of this thesis, we traced the history of this idea; in the second, we laid out a novel experimental apparatus for exploring its consequences at scale. In this third chapter, we employ our new tools to build up the foundations of convection theory from first principles, starting with basic fluid physics and boundary layer theory and ending with a deep interrogation of the conductive state and a massive numerical survey of the critical convective transition.

The modern geodynamicist is the lucky inheritor of two hundred years of advanced fluid dynamic theory, much of it derived by hand in the pre-computer age and presented lucidly and comprehensively in the excellent textbook literature on the subject [@Schubert2001-ea] [@Turcotte2014-by]. Instead of reproducing the work of past giants, it is our intention in this chapter - amongst other objectives - to present a complete, but not unduly detailed, map of how the basic intuitions of fluid physics can be developed into high-level formal constructs like the Rayleigh number, the Nusselt number, and Rayleigh-Benard convection. In contrast to classical approaches, which are predominantly analytical, we intend to test and demonstrate the merits of a 'data-driven' approach in which non-trivial symbolic relations are extracted from numerical data wherever possible. Our reasons for proceeding in this way are fourfold:

- To exhibit and validate the methodology that we will go on to employ throughout the rest of this research project: it is always wise to test new methods on known results before proceeding into the unknown.
- To produce bespoke and unconventional derivations of classic results that are more serviceable to a modeller's intuitions and which anticipate the peculiar needs of later chapters.
- To fill in certain gaps in the classic literature and unify all relevant theory into a single backbone framework to support the rest of the thesis.
- To experiment with alternative framings of core geophysics concepts that may be more accessible for those in the larger planetary science community.

It will be seen in due course how this sort of exercise - approaching well-trod terrain from a new direction - can bear surprising fruits.

# Foundations

This research program is predicated on the notion that the solid Earth can be understood as a viscous convecting fluid.

Labouring under this paradigm, the modern geodynamicist is the fortunate inheritor of two hundred years of advanced fluid dynamic theory and experimental data. Much of this legacy is owed to the pre-computing era, when painstakingly hand-derived equations and complicated practical experiments hewed out the foundations of the field. The last half century has built upon those foundations with sophisticated computer-driven numerical modelling work and vast new datasets from Earth and beyond - resources that would have astonished the forefathers of the field.

Despite this remarkable progress, there are ways in which we remain as much in the dark as Kelvin and his contemporaries, who in the 19th century struggled to fit conductive thermodynamics to the reality of an ancient Earth. The thermal history of the Earth and other planets remains speculative and controversial. Basic questions about fluid dynamics in Earth-like geometries remain unanswered. Most damning, the crowning jewel of the geosciences - plate tectonics - still wants for a compelling physical mechanism, which even the most advanced modern models are yet to provide. There is a great deal of work to be done within the paradigm the convecting Earth - and reasons to believe that a new paradigm is waiting in the wings to replace it.

When at first you don't succeed, it is worth reviewing your methods. In our review of the literature, we discovered a substantial bias towards small-run, high-resolution, scenario-specific models. A consequence of this preference is that we have tended to move on to new models somewhat prematurely, leaving old models underexplored.

The research program we are now embarked upon takes a classic mantle convection scenario and explores it broadly, deeply, and curiously. The intention is to not just query the model, but *complete* it - both for its own sake, and as a demonstration of the feasibility and fecundity of massive suite modelling in the 21st century.

In this chapter, we will build up our model from first principles, concentrating the relevant strands of classical and recent thinking to produce a powerful and versatile apparatus for mantle convection studies in the modern era.
